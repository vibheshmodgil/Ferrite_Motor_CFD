CFD method

Transient 3D conduction model in OpenFOAM 12: 8 solid regions, still air at 40 °C (natural convection + radiation), uniform 40 °C start, Design9795_1 losses with temperature-dependent DC copper. Energy conservation is verified to -0.4 % with the production settings; the earlier report's model lost 49 % of the heat.

Boundary conditions & losses

Caveat: emissivity. Every duty1–5 run used emissivity 0.7 on all outer faces, but the housing is bare machined aluminium (ε ≈ 0.1). Radiation carried about 60–70 % of the external heat loss in these runs, so all times to limit are optimistic, most for the long points (duty4, duty5) where the housing-to-ambient path sets the rise. duty4 is being re-run at ε 0.1; duty1, 2, 3 and 5 stay at ε 0.7 (user decision) and are flagged.
Model, assumptions and justification
Geometry / meshthe CFD mesh is the earlier v7 motor_65 geometry, 8 conformal solid regions, 2.39 M tetrahedra (gmsh). Against Design9795_1: stack 65 vs 64 mm and shaft 30 vs 25 mm; outer diameter, bore and rotor match, same 12-slot/10-pole spoke-ferrite layout. Losses are the Design9795_1 values applied to the v7 volumes, so heat density and heat capacity are off by ~1–2 % (about 1–2 % on run time). The detailed-geometry runs (section 5) check the winding model itself.
Start / ambientuniform 40 °C at t = 0; surroundings 40 °C (slide 3)
Outer convectionper-face CFD h, housing mean 4.0 (range 0.0–22.5) W/m²K over 72,137 faces; shaft mean 6.2 — from the 3D natural-convection CFD at ΔT 60 K (h ~ ΔT^0.25, not re-scaled); fallback uniform 6 W/m²K
Radiationgrey body ε = 0.7 to 40 °C, re-linearised every step (h_rad = εσ(Tw²+Ta²)(Tw+Ta)); fins see each other, so effective ε below 0.9 paint value
Air gap0.0005 m gap at r = 0.053 m: Ta = ω²r_mδ³/ν², Nu = 2 / 0.128Ta^0.367 / 0.409Ta^0.241, k_eff = (Nu/2)·0.03 W/m·K (Becker–Kaye, as in lumped motor-thermal tools)
End spacesh = 15 + 6.75 v^0.65 (v = rotor tip speed), applied as conducting air k = h·0.01 m; ρ×10 for stable coupling (air heat capacity ~12 J/K vs ~7 kJ/K motor). The internal CFD of the earlier report found the end-winding h about half this correlation, so this is optimistic for the end-turn hot spot.
Copper lossfrom the Design9795_1 FEMM model, spread uniformly over the winding: the DC part at 100 °C scaled per cell every step as q(1 + 0.00393(T − 100 °C)) (the hot winding makes more heat), plus the AC part (skin / proximity) as a constant source (it actually falls slightly with T, so holding it constant is slightly conservative)
Iron / mechanicalstator and rotor iron from the full-cycle FEMM iron-loss calculation with the Motor-CAD Steinmetz coefficients and build factor 1.6, uniform in each region; bearing friction into the bearings, windage into the internal air
Time stepimplicit Euler, fixed Δt per point (table in Scope); 2 coupling iterations for runs under 1 h, 1 for longer runs (make_case.py: SHORT = END_TIME < 3600)
Limitswinding hot spot (region maximum) 155 °C, magnet maximum 100 °C; time to limit = first crossing, linear between steps
Energy checkadiabatic 300 s test against the exact stored energy (section 2.4) and generated − out = stored per run (section 3)

Solver

OpenFOAM 12 foamMultiRun, solid module per region, implicit Euler, fixed time step per point (table above); region coupling iterated to make_case.py defaults: OUTER_TOL 1e-3 (housing 2e-2), up to 100 outer correctors, inner relTol 0.1, nNonOrth 0. Runs on 4 MPI ranks.

Verification

Before the fix, an adiabatic test (outer skin insulated, fixed losses, copper loss not temperature-dependent) with 299.76 W for 300 s must store exactly 89.93 kJ; the model stored only 46 kJ (−49 %). In the old 12 h continuous run ~336 W was generated, ~205 W left to ambient and only ~9 W was being stored, so ~120 W simply vanished — every old run time was too long by roughly a factor of two.

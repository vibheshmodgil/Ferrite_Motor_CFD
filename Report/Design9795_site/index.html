Results per duty point

Caveat: emissivity. Every duty1–5 run used emissivity 0.7 on all outer faces, but the housing is bare machined aluminium (ε ≈ 0.1). Radiation carried about 60–70 % of the external heat loss in these runs, so all times to limit are optimistic, most for the long points (duty4, duty5) where the housing-to-ambient path sets the rise. duty4 is being re-run at ε 0.1; duty1, 2, 3 and 5 stay at ε 0.7 (user decision) and are flagged.

Key findings

  1. duty1 meets its 5 min target: winding reaches 155 °C at 6 min 28 s (+29 %); copper makes 610 of 709 W.
  2. duty2 meets its 15 min target: winding reaches 155 °C at 19 min 03 s (+27 %); copper makes 317 of 452 W.
  3. duty3 misses its 30 min target: magnets reach 100 °C at 22 min 18 s (-26 %); the rotor side receives 74 W (rotor iron, bearings, windage) and has poor cooling paths, and AC copper is 39 W at 303 Hz.
  4. duty4 misses its 45 min target: magnets reach 100 °C at 14 min 11 s (-68 %); the rotor side receives 188 W (rotor iron, bearings, windage) and has poor cooling paths, and AC copper is 149 W at 778 Hz.
  5. Model: duty1 and duty4 use the detailed real-coil model; duty2, 3 and 5 use the baseline (simplified winding) model, which the detailed runs show is conservative (time to 155 °C duty1 +23 %, duty4 +6 % in the detailed model). In the detailed model the front and rear end windings stay within 1 °C; the baseline's rear hot spot is mostly a smeared-winding artefact, but the rear remains the weaker cooled end (sections 3.1 and 3.4).

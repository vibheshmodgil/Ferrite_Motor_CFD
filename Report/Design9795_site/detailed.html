Detailed geometry

The baseline model treats the winding as a homogenised copper-resin block. A second model with the real coils, resin, bobbins, busbars, straps and t-bars was run at duty1 and duty4 to check it. The baseline reaches every limit first, so it is conservative; it is used for all points.

Who heats whom: component heat budget, both models

Per part at a saved time, from the OpenFOAM interface heat flows: own loss (what the part makes), net heat received from its neighbours (+ = heated by others, − = passes heat on), heat to the surroundings, and stored = own + received + to surroundings (what warms the part). The interface flows cancel in total to within a few W. Stator and rotor have no flux monitor of their own; they take the mirror of their neighbours' flows.

Interpretation. The homogenised baseline is conservative for the hot spot: in the real coil, heat spreads along the continuous copper wire from the end turns into the slot, so the detailed end-turn peak is lower and 155 °C is reached later. The baseline slightly under-predicts the winding mean, because the detailed model resolves the resin / bobbin barrier between copper and iron. The global energy balance of the detailed runs stays within −0.8 … +0.3 %.

Verdict

Verdict. duty1: limit times +23 % in the detailed model; duty4: limit times +6 %, +6 % in the detailed model. In every case the baseline reaches the limit first, so it is conservative: close agreement at duty4, and a larger but safe-side margin at duty1, where the end-turn hot spot dominates. The baseline modelling is validated as a slightly conservative model, so duty2, duty3 and duty5, which have no detailed run, use the baseline mesh, while duty1 and duty4 report the detailed results. No detailed runs were made for duty2, duty3 and duty5: the detailed model will be redone later on the latest CAD.

Electromagnetic (FEMM)

Where the heat comes from. Losses per duty point from the FEMM model of Design9795_1, tuned to Motor-CAD: copper dominates at low speed (duty1 610 of 709 W); at top speed (duty4) the loss spreads over AC copper, stator iron and bearings, because the point sits on the voltage limit and needs heavy field-weakening current.

Why losses are high

duty1: Grade 12.3 % @ 23 km/h, 37 N·m @ 1782 rpm

Cause. Low speed, high torque: 293 A rms line for 37 N.m at 148 Hz with plenty of voltage margin (28.1 of 49.4 V). The id of -70 A pk is MTPA (reluctance torque of the spoke rotor), not field weakening. Copper loss goes with I^2: Cu DC 580 W is 82 % of the total. Cu AC stays 30 W because f is low (skin depth 6.2 mm vs wire 2.95 mm, d/delta 0.47), even though the slot field is the highest of all points. Iron is 81 W (tooth B up to 1.97 T) at only 148 Hz.

Thermal consequence. Heat is in the winding (580 W DC + 30 W AC, slot and end-winding): hottest spot is the coil / end winding; stator iron 63 W and rotor 18 W are minor.

Electromagnetic levers. Copper area / lower resistance (fill factor, shorter end turns), magnet grade or temperature (hotter ferrite = less Br = more current), MTPA is already least-current.

Voltage 28.1 of 49.4 V pk (with id = 0 it would need 31 V); current angle 17°, d-axis flux reduced 29 %, power factor 0.75.

duty2: Grade 17.6 % @ 16 km/h, 26.3 N·m @ 2550 rpm

Cause. 206 A rms line at 212 Hz (36.2 of 49.4 V, id -34 A pk, mostly MTPA). Cu DC 285 W falls with I^2 as torque drops while iron rises with frequency (106 W); Cu AC 32 W grows with f^2 (d/delta 0.57).

Thermal consequence. Winding still dominant (317 W) but stator iron (84 W) is significant: heat split winding > stator teeth/yoke.

Electromagnetic levers. As duty1; thinner lamination or a lower-loss grade starts to pay off.

duty3: Grade 25 % @ 11 km/h, 20 N·m @ 3637 rpm

Cause. 157 A rms line at 303 Hz (47.6 of 49.4 V, id -23 A pk, mostly MTPA). Cu DC 166 W falls with I^2 as torque drops while iron rises with frequency (143 W); Cu AC 39 W grows with f^2 (d/delta 0.68).

Thermal consequence. Winding 205 W, stator iron 115 W, mechanical 46 W: heat more evenly split; teeth and yoke start to matter for the housing path.

Electromagnetic levers. Voltage margin is nearly used (47.6 V): battery sag pushes toward field weakening and more current.

duty4: Flat road 60 km/h, 3.24 N·m @ 9339 rpm

Cause. Top speed, 778 Hz, at the voltage limit (49.2 of 49.4 V pk): holding 3.24 N.m needs id -96 A pk (98 % of the current is field-weakening d-axis current; with id = 0 the back-EMF would need 99 V). So 120 A rms line flows for only 3.24 N.m (pf 0.48), and its slot leakage field at 778 Hz gives Cu AC 149 W, more than the DC copper (97 W): skin depth 2.7 mm is now about the wire diameter 2.95 mm (d/delta 1.09). Iron (154 + 31 W) is high because loss/kg scales with f (hysteresis) and f^2 (eddy) even though the d-axis current cuts the main flux by 51 %; tooth-tip B still peaks at 1.91 T. Bearings + windage (156 W) are 27 % of the total.

Thermal consequence. Heat moves out of the copper DC term: AC copper (149 W) sits in the slot (worst near the slot opening, where leakage B is highest), stator iron 154 W in teeth/tips and yoke, rotor iron 31 W plus bearings 109 W and windage 47 W heat the rotor, shaft and bearings with poor cooling paths.

Electromagnetic levers. Cu AC: stranded/litz or smaller parallel strands (loss ~ d^4 per strand), keep conductors away from the slot opening, deeper or closed slot tips; iron: 0.2-0.27 mm lamination or better grade cuts eddy (~ t^2); voltage: higher DC link or less battery sag (48 V costs +38 W, 44 V +81 W, nearly all copper); control: loss-minimising instead of MTPA trades a little copper for iron.

Voltage 49.2 of 49.4 V pk (with id = 0 it would need 99 V); current angle 78°, d-axis flux reduced 51 %, power factor 0.48.

duty5: Grade 7 % @ 30 km/h, 12.43 N·m @ 1612 rpm

Cause. Light load at low speed (99 A rms, 134 Hz): every term is small. Cu DC 66 W still dominates; iron 38 W is mostly magnet-field hysteresis at 134 Hz; Cu AC 3 W (d/delta 0.45).

Thermal consequence. Low total (123 W); winding first, stator iron second. Thermally benign.

Electromagnetic levers. No action needed on the EM side.
